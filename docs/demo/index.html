How far to trust it

  • Model: Ours-lite, EfficientNet-B4 with three heads (17.6 M parameters), trained on FF++, two commercial retouching services split into single operations, and part-level forgeries with exact edit regions. The paper's main model uses a CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone (306 M parameters), too large for a browser.
  • Where the forgery is: on held-out part forgeries in FF++ video frames the edited part is named correctly in 98–99 % of detections, and restoring that part to the original removes 87–88 % of them (ceiling 88 %). On high-quality still photos such as the examples above, this model detects far fewer part forgeries (38–64 %; the paper's larger model 60–92 %). The part names on this page match the Python implementation on 128 of 128 test crops.
  • Which retouching operation: reliable for smoothing; eye enlargement and face reshaping are often missed on services the model has not seen.
  • Known weaknesses: some untouched photos are called filter; about 40 % of genuine Celeb-DF video frames are called fake, and strong JPEG compression raises that rate on genuine faces. The sentence is a template filled from the model's outputs; how much was edited is not estimated.
  • The face is cropped from 478 MediaPipe landmarks (bounding box + 35 % per side). Probabilities are raw model outputs, not calibrated confidence.
