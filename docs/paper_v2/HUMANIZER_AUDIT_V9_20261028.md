## Verdict

On the six-feature checklist the text is clean: no hype, no adverb-comma openers, no self-congratulation, no em-dashes, two coined phrases, and the repetition that exists is almost all the conventional abstract / contributions / Finding / conclusion echo of the same seven claims, which a reader of an IEEE paper expects. The vocabulary, hedging and verb strength are those of a careful measurement paper, and every number is pointed at a table or a supplement reference.

What still reads as machine-assisted is rhythm rather than vocabulary: a run of 50–86-word sentences built from colon + semicolon + "and" + "so", broken by two-to-four-word punch sentences ("The escape is not.", "The loss is general.", "Four are real.", "The operation is smoothing."), and the same seven key clauses recurring in nearly the same words five times each. An attentive reviewer would not call the paper AI-written from its word choice, but could from its cadence. Applying rows 1–12 (vary the body-internal repeats, leave the abstract and Findings), rows 15–20 (fold the punch sentences in) and rows 21–33 (split the thirteen longest sentences) removes that signal without touching a number, a citation or the strength of any claim.

Overall: **does not read as AI-written on the six-feature test; reads as AI-assisted on cadence (long chains plus punch fragments plus repeated key clauses). Low risk; 34 wording-only items proposed, none changes a claim.**
