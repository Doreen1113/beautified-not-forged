\subsection{Beautification hurts every detector, in both directions}
\label{sec:measure}
Table~\ref{tab:twosided} scores seventeen published detectors and ours by the rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}. Two
detectors were added since the first version of this measurement: CADDM~\cite{caddm}, whose released EfficientNet-B4
checkpoint reproduces its published Celeb-DF-v2 video AUC on the authors' own crop (0.937 against 0.939), and
LAA-Net~\cite{laanet}, whose released SBI checkpoint is one point short of its published number on its own crop (0.943
against 0.954) and needs that crop, so we rebuild it inside our face box before scoring. Both are run with the authors'
code, released weights and test-time preprocessing; the setup record lists every deviation.

\textbf{Commercial retouching.} We begin with the service, because it is where the published detectors fail most and
where the fixed-threshold rule understates the failure. Table~\ref{tab:v4retouch} reads the 2,210 untouched FFHQ
originals and their renders at the strongest skin smoothing (Smoothing-90) in two ways. The first needs no threshold:
for each pair, is the render scored more forged than its own original, and what are the two medians of
$P(\text{fake})$? Fifteen of the seventeen published detectors score the smoothed render above its original in
89--99\% of pairs. The medians show the size of the move: Xception from 0.29 to 0.94, F3Net from 0.13 to 0.97, UCF from
0.03 to 0.98, SBI from 0.21 to 0.99, GenD~\cite{gend2026} from 0.23 to 0.92, LAA-Net from 0.23 to 0.97. For these
detectors a heavily smoothed genuine face is scored like a forgery, whatever threshold is chosen. The two exceptions are
NPR, whose scores sit at 1.0 for originals and renders alike and which is excluded, and CADDM, which scores the render
above its original in 60.9\% of pairs with medians 0.54 and 0.75. CADDM is trained to localise swap artefacts with an
anchor head and to suppress identity, so its evidence is a boundary and identity cue rather than skin texture, and
smoothing moves it least.

The second reading is the rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}: the threshold that accuses 5\% of the service's own originals.
Thirteen of the seventeen detectors then accuse 36.3--68.7\% of the Smoothing-90 renders (GenD 68.7\%, DFD-HR~\cite{dfdhr2026}
64.9\%, SRM 62.8\%, SBI 37.3\%, EfficientNet-B4 36.3\%), UnivFD 16.6\%, LAA-Net 17.2\%, CADDM 6.3\%. The 5\%-FPR
column is the smaller number for several detectors because they are near saturation on the service's stills: the
threshold that leaves 5\% of originals above it is 0.99 or higher for SBI, UCF, EfficientNet-B4, LAA-Net and CADDM, so a
render can move from a median of 0.23 to 0.97, as LAA-Net's does, and still fall under a threshold of 0.992. The
threshold-free column is therefore the one to read first; the fixed threshold is kept because it is the operating rule
of every other source in this paper. Over all 26,519 renders of the four operations at three levels, the detectors
accuse 5.6--16.8\% at the same threshold, with the AUROC of render against original at 0.52--0.60, because the other
three operations of the service (whitening, face slimming, eye enlarging) are not accused above the 5\% floor; the
retouching result is a smoothing result, and the pooled number is not the headline. Published detectors are scored on
one shared crop here, not each under its own preprocessing as on the video sources (Sec.~\ref{sec:detect}), and the
threshold is set on the service's originals; at a fixed threshold of 0.5 the sixteen usable detectors already call
between 1\% (UnivFD) and 51\% (CADDM) of the untouched originals fake, which is a calibration problem of FF++-trained
detectors on FFHQ stills that the 5\%-FPR rule hides.

Our full-data three-way model accuses 8.6\% of the Smoothing-90 renders and 4.7\% of all renders at the 5\%-FPR rule
(second seed 12.5\% and 5.5\%), scores the render above its original in 32.5\% of pairs, and has medians of 0.002 and
0.001. The binary control trained on the same data gives 9.1\%, 5.2\% and 70.5\%, so the third output class is not what
removes the accusation. The FF++-only exposure arms in the lower block of the table are read in
Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism2}.

\textbf{App presets and colour filters.} On Celeb-DF-B every published detector loses AUC under the app presets, and the
loss is largest for the detectors that detect best: SBI from 0.875 to 0.752, Effort from 0.909 to 0.807, Forensics
Adapter from 0.909 to 0.828, GenD from 0.924 to 0.807, DFD-HR from 0.938 to 0.838, CADDM from 0.886 to 0.762 and
LAA-Net from 0.889 to 0.698, the largest drop of the seventeen. For CADDM, Libourel et al.~\cite{libourel2024} report
0.91 to 0.76 on the same benchmark, so the two measurements agree. With re-encoding subtracted, the presets alone raise
the miss rate by 21.8 (SBI), 11.0 (Effort), 15.2 (Forensics Adapter), 12.0 (GenD), 3.6 (DFD-HR) and 14.7 (LAA-Net)
points; for CADDM the rise under re-encoding alone (13.9) exceeds the rise under the preset (11.0), so its
beautification-specific rise is $-2.9$ and its loss is a re-encoding loss. The false-alarm side differs by detector:
on beautified genuine frames DFD-HR accuses 14.6\%, CADDM 13.8\%, GenD 9.9\%, Forensics Adapter 7.9\%, LAA-Net 6.0\%,
Effort 5.4\% and SBI 1.8\%, against 5\% on untreated frames. All four CLIP detectors of 2025--26 lose 0.08--0.12 AUC
under the presets, and their errors fall on different sides: DFD-HR and GenD accuse beautified genuine faces, Effort and
Forensics Adapter let beautified deepfakes through. The same two CLIP detectors fall from 0.88--0.89 to 0.81 under the
Celeb-DF-v2 filters, where their miss rates rise by 23.3 and 11.9 points, and from 0.94 to 0.90--0.92 under the DFD
filters, where Effort's miss rate rises by 15.1 points [11.1, 18.6]. The false-alarm side is small for these two on
colour filters and large for UnivFD (+11.6 on DFD) and NPR, whose scores collapse under any filter.

Two details matter for reading Table~\ref{tab:twosided}. Where the filter is applied matters: with the filter on the
face crop rather than on the full frame, a vignette is centred on the face and Forensics Adapter loses 0.017 more AUC
on DFD; we report the full-frame version and keep the crop-level one in the supplement. And a fixed threshold sees
what AUC cannot: our full-data three-way model keeps its AUC under the DFD filters (0.942 to 0.931) while its miss rate
rises by 8.9 points, because the filters shift its scores for genuine and forged frames alike. We report both numbers
for every model, and on the retouching service the threshold-free pair statistics as well.
