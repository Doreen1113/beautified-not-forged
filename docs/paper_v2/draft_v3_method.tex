\section{Problem and Evaluation Rule}
\label{sec:protocol}
A face image $x$ is \emph{forged} if it shows a person or an action that was not captured: an identity swap, a reenactment,
a synthesised face, or a part replaced by another person's or by generated content. It is \emph{beautified} if its
appearance was edited after capture while identity, expression, pose and speech stay those of the capture: colour and tone,
skin texture, or a smooth reshaping of the person's own features. The two attributes are independent; a beautified forgery
is forged. The tool does not decide: a retouching service that uses a neural network beautifies, and a reenactment that
keeps the identity forges. Table~\ref{tab:labels} lists cases near the boundary. A detector has to return a forgery score
that does not move when a face is beautified, and the beautified attribute is reported separately for the user.

\textbf{One rule for every detector.} Each detector is scored by its probability of forgery, $P(\text{fake})$. On every
source we set the threshold at a false-alarm rate of 5\% on the untreated genuine frames, keep it fixed, and report under
beautification the false-alarm rate FA (genuine faces called fake) and the miss rate MISS (forgeries not called fake), with
the AUC of untreated and of beautified pairs. A forgery that a three-way model sends to its beautified class is a miss. An
earlier version of this work counted only forgeries called \emph{real} as misses, which hid 19.5\% of the beautified
Celeb-DF-B deepfakes that our model sent to the third class; that rule is withdrawn. Confidence intervals are 95\%
percentile intervals over 1,000 (Celeb-DF-B) or 500 bootstrap resamples of videos, and differences between detectors use
identical resamples, so their intervals are paired.

\textbf{Sources.} Celeb-DF-B~\cite{libourel2024}: 231 Celeb-DF-v2 videos, each as recorded, re-encoded, and beautified by
one of four app presets, for genuine and synthesised faces (32 frames per video). Re-encoding alone changes the scores, so
the rise in MISS attributed to beautification is the rise under the preset minus the rise under re-encoding. Celeb-DF-v2
filters: the 516 test videos of Celeb-DF-v2~\cite{celebdf}, 32 frames each, every frame also saved under one of eleven
colour filters~\cite{pilgram} that no model of ours trained on, assigned per video. DFD filters: the 332 DFD
videos~\cite{dfd}, 32 frames each, under one of sixteen filters from a second library, grayscale and sepia, none used in
training or in the diagnosis that motivated the method; the set was built after the method was fixed. Commercial
retouching: 2,210 FFHQ originals and their renders by a service never seen in training (Sec.~\ref{sec:ali}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{A bounded operator over beautification}
\label{sec:operator}
We write identity-preserving appearance edits as a differentiable operator $B(x;u)$ on a face crop $x\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$
with parameters $u$ in an unconstrained space, every component mapped by a sigmoid into a bounded range:
a per-channel tone curve (gamma, gain and lift), a $3\times3$ colour matrix close to the identity, saturation from
monochrome to 1.7, contrast about the mean, a smooth spatial field on a $4\times4$ grid that scales luminance and tints each
channel (vignettes, light leaks and tint gradients), a blend toward a Gaussian-smoothed copy (skin smoothing), and additive
grain. Table~\ref{tab:operator_params} lists the components and ranges. The operator contains no geometric warp and no
face-aware mask, so it cannot express face slimming or eye enlargement; these are covered by the commercial renders of
Sec.~\ref{sec:data}.

\textbf{Range fitted to measured edits.} The ranges are not chosen by hand. For 1,000 pairs each of (original, render) from
two retouching services and of (frame, filtered frame) for the training colour filters and random grading, we fit $u$ by
gradient descent on the pixel error. The operator reproduces the colour filters at 39.8\,dB PSNR and explains 7\,dB of a
Tencent render (21.4 to 28.6\,dB); the remainder is geometry, which a photometric operator cannot express
(Table~\ref{tab:operator}). Where the 99th percentile of the fitted parameters met a hand-set bound, the bound was widened,
and the search below is confined to the 0.5th--99.5th percentile range of the fitted edits and starts from a fitted edit.
A Gaussian fit was tried first and rejected because it excluded monochrome, which the measured filters reach.

\subsection{Robust training against the worst edit}
\label{sec:robust}
Let $f_\theta$ be the detector and $\ell$ the cross-entropy of its forgery logit. We minimise
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\Big[\ell\big(f_\theta(x),y\big)+\max_{u\in U}\ell\big(f_\theta(B(x;u)),y\big)\Big],
\label{eq:robust}
\end{equation}
where $U$ is the fitted range and $y$ the forgery label, which the edit does not change. The inner maximisation is one
signed-gradient step of size 1 on $u$ from a measured edit (R+FGSM~\cite{tramer2018}; a three-step variant is reported),
followed by projection onto $U$. The step costs one extra forward and backward pass. The beautified attribute of the edited
view is set to one. The edit is applied to the genuine and the forged item of every training step with the same
probability, so that no edit is more frequent under either label: in our earlier runs, random grading applied more often to
non-forged items taught the model that a graded face is less likely forged, and the filters then raised its miss rate rather
than its false alarms (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

Equation~\ref{eq:robust} is adversarial training~\cite{madry2018} with a perturbation set that is not an $\ell_p$ ball but
the edits a user can apply. Two controls separate the search from the operator: the same operator with a random $u$ at
every step, and the worst of four random draws chosen by loss~\cite{fatima2025}. Single-step training can overfit to its
own step~\cite{wong2020fast}; we therefore report the accuracy of every model under a ten-step search as well.

\subsection{Supervision from paired edits}
\label{sec:data}
The operator of Sec.~\ref{sec:operator} covers photometric edits. Geometric and face-aware edits, and the
beautifications a model should recognise as such, come from paired data in which every edited image has a pixel-aligned
original $x_0$, so that the footprint of the edit is known exactly, $M(x)=\mathrm{dilate}_\rho(\mathbf{1}[\|x-x_0\|_\infty>\tau])$
with $\tau=6$ on 8-bit intensities.

\textbf{FF++ triplets.} FF++ c23~\cite{ffpp} under the official 720/140/140 video split. For each genuine training frame
$x_0$ a step draws a genuine item, a beautified item (one of four landmark and skin operations at a random strength, one of
14 colour filters~\cite{pilgram}, or a random grading) and a forged item (a self-blend of $x_0$~\cite{sbi}, an observed
forgery from the four FF++ methods, or a part-level forgery). The same random blur or downscale is applied to all three
items, so that sharpness does not predict the label. The FF++-only models use nothing else.

\textbf{Commercial renders.} 7,831 FFHQ photographs~\cite{ffhq} and their renders by two retouching services from
RetouchingFFHQ~\cite{retouchingffhq}, Tencent and Megvii, which apply eye enlargement, face lifting, whitening and
smoothing together. Each render $R$ of an original $O$ is split into single-operation components in $O$'s geometry: dense
optical flow~\cite{dis} on locally normalised luminance gives the warp $f$, the photometric residual is
$P=R\circ(\mathrm{id}-f^{-1})-O$, and the components are the eye and contour warps of $O$ by the masked flow and the
low- and high-frequency parts of $P$ added to $O$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:decomp}); applying the four in sequence reproduces $R$ at
108\,dB PSNR. Components whose measured edit falls under the noise floor are dropped, leaving 50,112 of 73,952. The
decomposition also lets a measured commercial edit be transplanted onto a forgery: the photometric residual of a render is
added to a self-blend of the same original, so that the same commercial edit appears on genuine and forged faces. The third
service, Alibaba, is used only for testing.

\textbf{Part-level forgeries.} 61,510 training forgeries on FF++ frames transplant the eyes, nose or mouth of another
identity or regenerate the part with Stable Diffusion inpainting; pixels outside the feathered mask are copied from $x_0$,
so the footprint is exact. The held-out test set uses test-split frames and donors from test videos only.

\subsection{Model}
\label{sec:model}
The detector is a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{clip} with rank-16 LoRA~\cite{lora} on every attention projection (3.2\,M
trained parameters of 306\,M) and a linear forgery head on the pooled feature. A second linear head gives the beautified
attribute and a $1\times1$ convolution on the spatial tokens gives the evidence map trained against the exact footprints;
\pending{in the final model these auxiliary heads read detached features, so they report on the representation without
shaping it (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}); the three-way output real / fake / filter is derived from the two logits}. The
faithfulness test of the evidence map restores the named region to the original and measures how often the decision
changes, against the true footprint as ceiling (Sec.~\ref{sec:explain}). Training: 224\,px crops, AdamW at $10^{-4}$ with
warm-up and cosine decay, 8 epochs, a batch of 16 genuine bases with their forged and beautified items. The checkpoint is
chosen on validation data only (FF++ validation macro-F1 for FF++-only models; for full-data models the mean of FF++
validation macro-F1 and balanced accuracy on held-out originals and renders of the training services). No test source is
used for any choice.
