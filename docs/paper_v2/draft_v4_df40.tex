\subsection{Forgery methods newer than FF++: DF40}
\label{sec:df40}
The untreated comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:detect} covers face swaps on Celeb-DF-v2 and DFD. DF40~\cite{df40} adds
nine face-swap and twelve face-reenactment methods newer than those of FF++, in an FF++ domain and a Celeb-DF domain,
with released 256\,px crops. We fixed the evaluation before any detector was run on it: the official test lists with
the 718 MobileSwap videos built from FF++ training identities removed, all FF++-domain videos, 140 fake videos per method
in the Celeb-DF domain, video-level AUC against the same-domain real videos, the mean over methods per family, and
paired differences of the main model minus each CLIP detector on identical resamples. No bar was attached, because
no method claim rests on it, and no beautification is applied. Table~\ref{tab:v4df40} gives the result for twelve
published detectors and our models.

On face swap the main model is on par with the CLIP detectors: 0.952 in the FF++ domain against Effort 0.944,
Forensics Adapter 0.948 and GenD 0.966 (paired differences $+0.007$ [$-0.002$, $+0.018$], $+0.004$ [$-0.006$, $+0.012$]
and $-0.015$ [$-0.023$, $-0.007$]), and 0.905 in the Celeb-DF domain against 0.935, 0.897 and 0.947. On reenactment it is
behind every CLIP detector: 0.718 and 0.502 against Effort 0.808 and 0.710, Forensics Adapter 0.828 and 0.665, GenD
0.935 and 0.828 and DFD-HR 0.932 and 0.698, with paired differences of $-0.090$ to $-0.218$ in the FF++ domain and
$-0.163$ to $-0.326$ in the Celeb-DF domain, every interval below zero. The second seed is 0.780 and 0.559. On four
Celeb-DF-domain reenactment methods (TPSM, DaNet, MCNet, FaceVid2Vid) the main model scores 0.27--0.37, below chance.
A three-way read-out on 1,200 frames per cell shows where the frames go: reenactment frames are called real in
49--51\% and beautified in 21--33\%, forged in only 18--29\%, while face-swap frames are called forged in 70--74\%. The
claim of Sec.~\ref{sec:detect} therefore holds on Celeb-DF-v2, DFD and DF40 face swap, and fails on DF40 reenactment. A
whole-face neural rendering has no blending boundary and a smooth skin texture, which is what our additions teach the
model to read as genuine or beautified. The main model is not a general-purpose reference detector for forgeries of
every kind; its scope is the swap and part-level families it was trained on, under beautification.

\textbf{The loss follows smoothing exposure.} After the first table was read, we scored the exposure arms of
Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism2} on the same fixed list; this is a post hoc extension and selected nothing. The arms order on
reenactment the way they order on commercial smoothing, in reverse (Table~\ref{tab:v4tradeoff}): P0 0.906 and 0.692
(second seed 0.877 and 0.720); P2 without smoothing 0.858 and 0.705; P2 0.809 and 0.644 (0.785 and 0.643); P2 smoothing
only 0.779 and 0.593 (0.833 and 0.629); P1 0.807 and 0.569; the three-way FF++-only model 0.809 and 0.637; the binary
full-data control 0.842 and 0.647; the main model 0.718 and 0.502. Face swap moves little across the same arms
(0.94--0.98 and 0.88--0.94). Views without smoothing cost about 0.05 in the FF++ domain and nothing in the Celeb-DF
domain; adding smoothing costs a further 0.05--0.08 and 0.06--0.11; the third class, the external genuine stills and the
commercial renders of the full-data model cost the rest. The symmetric smoothing arm is

The same trade-off is visible in published detectors. GenD and DFD-HR are the two strongest on reenactment (0.935 and
0.932 in the FF++ domain) and the two that score a commercially smoothed face above its own original most often
(98.5 and 99.0\% of pairs, Table~\ref{tab:v4retouch}); Effort and Forensics Adapter, which accuse fewer smoothed faces at
the fixed threshold, are 0.10--0.13 lower on reenactment in that domain. A detector that reads smooth skin as evidence detects
neural rendering and accuses retouching; one that has learned not to loses the rendering. We do not claim that this is
the only route to reenactment detection, only that every arm of ours and the published detectors we ran lie on it.

Four caveats attach to the table. The real videos are the FF++ and Celeb-DF-v2 test reals already used elsewhere in
this paper, and Celeb-DF has no identity-disjoint split. Every detector in the table, ours included, is trained on
FF++. Our models were trained on a different crop from the released 256\,px DF40 crops; the size of that handicap on
Celeb-DF-v2 and DFD is given in the supplement. SBI is absent because its pipeline needs its own crop from full
frames, and CADDM and LAA-Net are not scored on this set. DF40's face-editing set (five GAN and diffusion attribute
editors on CelebA) contains no beautification or retouching, so DF40 does not test the beautified class, and we do not
use it for that.
