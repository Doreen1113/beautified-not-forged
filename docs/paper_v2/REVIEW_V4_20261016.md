## 1. Summary of the claims

C1 (measurement). Seventeen published detectors are scored with one rule: score P(fake), threshold at 5% FPR on the
untreated genuine frames of each source, FA and MISS both reported. Under app presets (Celeb-DF-B) every detector that
detects untreated forgeries loses AUC, and the four 2025–26 CLIP detectors lose 0.08–0.12. Under colour filters
(Celeb-DF-v2 with 11 filters, DFD with 16) the CLIP detectors lose AUC and Effort's miss rate rises 15–23 points.

C2 (commercial smoothing). On 2,210 FFHQ originals and their renders by an unseen service (Alibaba, from
RetouchingFFHQ), 15 of the 17 detectors score the strongest-smoothing render above its own original in 89–99% of pairs,
and 13 accuse 36–69% of those renders at the 5%-FPR threshold of the originals. Whitening, slimming and eye enlarging stay
near the floor. The retouching result is a skin-smoothing result.

C3 (mechanism). In an FF++-only CLIP model, the two held-out filters with a radial luminance overlay move a genuine
face's forgery logit 3–10 times further than pure colour looks. The shift lies in a subspace that also carries forgery
evidence, so linear erasure fails.

C4 (exposure sets the sign of the error). With architecture, data and recipe fixed, giving beautified views to genuine
faces only (P1) raises misses on beautified deepfakes, while giving them to both classes (P2) moves errors toward false
alarms. Separability does not change (3 seeds, plus 2 seeds under Effort's tuning scheme for P0/P2).

C5 (pre-registered smoothing chain). Smoothing in the views is necessary and sufficient to stop the accusation of
commercially smoothed faces. The genuine-only recipe is an evasion route, and a class-asymmetric smoothing form is learned
as a label. A class-symmetric smoothing view is the only recipe that neither accuses nor is fooled, at a cost in misses
under heavy smoothing and in DF40 reenactment. This holds for LoRA tuning of CLIP but not for Effort's scheme.

C6 (detector). A CLIP ViT-L/14 + LoRA model trained with beautification on both classes, decomposed commercial renders and
part-level forgeries is said to be on par with Effort and Forensics Adapter on untreated Celeb-DF-v2, DFD and DF40 face swap,
behind them on DF40 reenactment, and 0.04–0.07 AUC better under app presets. It accuses 8.6% of Smoothing-90 renders.

C7 (explanation). An evidence head supervised with exact footprints reaches IoU7 0.83–0.84 on held-out part-level
forgeries. Restoring the region it names removes 93.5–94.0% of detections, against 93.6–94.1% for the true footprint.

Plus a negative-results section (operator adversarial training, learned inverse, map-pooled decision, third class) and
score fusion with CADDM, which moves the operating point but does not escape the trade-off.

## 2. Overall assessment

The measurement (C1, C2) is careful, unusually honest, and the most publishable part. The paired, threshold-free statistic
on commercial smoothing is a clean result. It is controlled by the null result on the service's other three operations,
which go through the same rendering pipeline. The pre-registration discipline in the smoothing chain is a real strength.

The paper's problems are of four kinds:
1. Several headline sentences in the abstract, introduction and conclusion are contradicted by the paper's own tables.
2. The "mechanism" explains colour filters, not the smoothing failure the paper leads with.
3. The detector contribution is weak: the main model is dominated by its own binary control, is at chance on Celeb-DF-domain
   reenactment, and the recipe that fixes its shortcut opens a 34-point evasion route that the abstract does not mention.
4. The class definition is stated cleanly but is not operationalised. One clause contradicts "the tool does not decide".

None of this invalidates the measurement. Together, though, it makes the current version read as a detector paper whose
detector does not hold up, rather than as the strong measurement-and-shortcut paper it could be.

## 3. Problems

Severity: **fatal** = would cause rejection or desk rejection on its own if left as is; **major** = a reviewer will list
it as a reason to reject or ask for major revision; **minor** = should be fixed, will not decide the outcome.

### A. Problem definition and the class boundary

**A1. Major — the definition is not operationalised, and one clause contradicts another.**
Location: Sec. 3, lines 190–196; Table S1 (`tab:labels`).
- What a reviewer would say: "Forged = a part replaced by another person's or by generated content". "Beautified =
  appearance edited … skin texture … smooth reshaping". "The tool does not decide: a retouching service that uses a neural
  network beautifies". Take a commercial AI-skin or teeth-whitening tool that regenerates the skin or mouth with a
  generative model. By the 'part replaced by generated content' clause it is forged; by the 'tool does not decide' clause it
  is beautified. Your own SD-1.5 inpainting of the person's own mouth or nose is labelled forged even though identity and
  expression are kept. The criterion is semantic, but nothing measures identity, expression or pose preservation.
- Evidence: Sec. 3 text; part-level forgeries include "regenerate the part with Stable Diffusion inpainting" (line 259) and
  are labelled forged. Alibaba/Tencent/Megvii renders (neural pipelines, per CLAUDE.md dataset notes) are labelled beautified.
- Fix: (i) Define the boundary by what changes: identity, expression/action units, speech/mouth motion, and content that was
  not captured (an object or part that was not there). Do not define it by "generated content". (ii) Operationalise it.
  Report, for every training and test edit family, ArcFace (or similar) cosine similarity to the original, landmark/AU
  displacement, and the share of pixels changed. Show that the beautified families (scripted ops, three services, app presets,
  colour filters) fall on one side and part swaps and inpainting on the other. If own-part inpainting falls on the beautified
  side, say so and either relabel it or justify keeping it forged (e.g. "content not captured"). This is one day of compute
  on existing pairs. It turns a definition into a measurable criterion.

**A2. Major — the boundary table is thin and is not referenced from the main text.**
Location: line 195 ("The supplement lists cases near the boundary") with no `\ref`; Table S1 has 8 rows.
- What a reviewer would say: "The paper's thesis is a boundary. Missing cases include lip-sync / dubbing; age and gender
  editing; GAN/diffusion attribute editing (DF40's own face-editing subset, which you dismiss in Sec. S9 as 'no
  beautification'); relighting; virtual make-up transfer; generative face restoration / super-resolution (GFPGAN, CodeFormer,
  phone 'AI enhance'); generative AR filters that re-render the face (you exclude AR for a data reason, not a definitional
  one); morphing (a biometric case IJCB/TIFS reviewers will ask about); background replacement; and face swap followed by
  beautification vs beautification followed by swap. 'Expression editing; synthesised face: not tested' is
  wrong: DF40's entire-face-synthesis and face-editing sets exist and are on your disk."
- Evidence: Table S1; Sec. S9 last sentence; CLAUDE.md lists DF40 EFS folders (sd2.1, DiT, SiT, ddim, pixart) on disk.
- Fix: Expand Table S1 to about 15 rows with three columns: label under the definition, the operational test from A1, and
  whether it is evaluated. Move a 5-row version into the main paper (Sec. 3). Score the DF40 face-editing and
  entire-face-synthesis subsets (hours; frames and scorers exist) and report them under the label the definition assigns.

**A3. Major — by the paper's own requirement the main model fails.**
Location: line 196 ("A detector has to return a forgery score that does not move when a face is beautified");
Table II (`tab_v4_retouch_main`).
- What a reviewer would say: "You state invariance as the requirement. Then you show the main model scores the smoothed
  render *below* its original in 67.5% of 2,210 pairs (AUROC render vs original 0.371). That is a strong, significant
  shift: a sign test on 2,210 pairs is decisive. Only the FF++-only symmetric arm (53.7 / 51.8%) is invariant. Your band of
  25–75% (Addendum 34) is far too wide to call 32.5% 'invariant'."
- Evidence: `tab_v4_retouch.tex` (Ours 32.5%, AUROC 0.371; variant 30.0%, 0.375; P2-symmetric 53.7%, 0.488).
- Fix: Report the median paired logit shift with a bootstrap CI and a sign-test p-value for every model. State plainly that
  the full-data models move scores toward genuine under smoothing. Connect this to B3 (evasion).

**A4. Major — the title and abstract promise separation; the paper delivers measurement plus a recipe.**
Location: title; lines 141–154, 334–339; Sec. S11.
- What a reviewer would say: "'Separating beautification from face forgery' suggests the beautified class works. It sends
  20.8% of untouched originals to 'beautified'; balanced accuracy is 73.5% (target 75% missed); the beautified-class
  AUROC is 0.80–0.82; and the class 'does not improve detection'. A user-facing label that falsely says one in five
  untouched photos was retouched is itself an accusation."
- Evidence: Sec. S11; `tab_alipair.tex`.
- Fix: Retitle around the measurement and the shortcut (e.g. "Beautified Is Not Forged: How Face-Forgery Detectors Learn to
  Accuse Retouching"). Present the three-way output as an optional attribute with its error rates. Do not present it as a
  separation result. Also report the beautified-class rate on Celeb-DF-B `real_beautified` (does the app beautification get
  recognised?). It is cheap and currently missing.

**A5. Minor — the one-rule metric reads P(fake) only, so the "filter" class is evaluated nowhere in the main paper.**
Consistent with the definition, but say so in Sec. 3 in one sentence. Add the one supplement pointer (S11) for the native
reading.

### B. Reasoning chain

**B1. Major — the mechanism section explains colour filters, but the paper leads with smoothing.**
Location: abstract lines 55–57; Sec. 4.2 (lines 354–365); conclusion lines 571–572 ("The damage has a measurable
mechanism, a luminance ramp around the face read as forgery evidence").
- What a reviewer would say: "Your largest failure (36–69% accusation, 89–99% of pairs) is commercial skin smoothing,
  which adds no luminance ramp. The probe covers 2 of 11 colour filters (earlybird, mayfair). Nothing in Sec. 4.2 explains
  why smoothing reads as forgery, and your own smoothness probe 'found no feature-level handle' (line 563). Also, the probed
  model is not a published detector. It is your FF++-only three-way model (`clipe5f`), which was trained with beautified views
  (random grading and 14 pilgram filters), so the 'training-time beautification subspace' exists only because you put it there."
- Evidence: FINDINGS 2026-10-07 "Shift-subspace probe … FF++-only checkpoint (clipe5f)"; conclusion sentence above;
  Limitations lines 562–566.
- Further evidence that the mechanism does not transfer: on the same Celeb-DF-v2 filters, Effort's errors go the *other*
  way (ΔFA −2.8, ΔMISS +23.3; `tab_v4_twosided`). The probe's direction (genuine pushed toward forged) holds for GenD
  (ΔFA +9.9) but not for Effort or Forensics Adapter.
- Fix: (i) Narrow the claim: "for colour filters, a radial luminance overlay …". Remove "the damage has a measurable
  mechanism" from the abstract and conclusion. (ii) Run the probe on released weights. Effort, Forensics Adapter, GenD and
  DFD-HR all have a linear head on a pooled CLIP feature, so this takes hours. (iii) Add an intervention: a vignette-only
  overlay at several strengths, and the colour part of earlybird/mayfair without the vignette. Two filters are an anecdote; a
  dose-response is a mechanism. (iv) Run the same shift analysis for face-local bilateral smoothing on the service's
  originals. If smoothing moves along w for published detectors, that is the mechanism the paper actually needs.

**B2. Major — "the reenactment loss follows smoothing exposure" omits the arm that falsifies it.**
Location: lines 492–505; Table III (`tab_v4_tradeoff`); Sec. S9.
- What a reviewer would say: "The proposed mechanism is that 'a whole-face neural rendering has no blending boundary and a
  smooth skin texture, which is what our additions teach the model to read as genuine'. The arm taught most strongly that
  smooth means genuine is P1 smoothing-only. It accuses 0.7–1.0% of smoothed renders and passes 100% of
  bilateral-smoothed forgeries, yet its FF++-domain reenactment AUC is 0.871 / 0.869 / 0.900, indistinguishable from P0
  (0.906 / 0.877 / 0.873). This arm is in Table III but is left out of the ordering in the text (lines 498–499). Across the 13
  published detectors with both numbers, the share of renders above their own original does not significantly predict
  reenactment AUC either. The claim rests on GenD and DFD-HR, two of four CLIP detectors chosen after looking."
- Evidence: `tab_v4_df40.tex` (P1 smoothing only rows); my Spearman correlations over the 13 published detectors that have
  both numbers (Xception … CADDM, values from `tab_v4_retouch_main` and `tab_v4_df40_main`):
  above-own-original vs FR FF++ domain ρ = 0.36 (p = 0.23), vs FR Celeb-DF domain ρ = 0.48 (p = 0.10); accusation at 5% FPR vs
  FR FF++ ρ = 0.62 (p = 0.024, uncorrected, one of four tests), vs FR Celeb-DF ρ = 0.31 (p = 0.31). Without CADDM: 0.24, 0.34,
  0.57, 0.12. SRM has the highest above-own-original rate (99.3%) and a middling FR (0.830).
- Fix: Report every arm in the ordering, including P1o and the seed spread (P2o seed 2 is 0.833; P2-symmetric seeds are
  0.871 / 0.796). Replace "the loss follows smoothing exposure" with a descriptive statement: the both-class arms and
  the full-data models lose reenactment, and P1o does not. Drop the "smooth texture read as genuine" mechanism or test it.
  Report the cross-detector correlation with its p-value rather than picking two detectors. The defensible observation is
  that the main model's three-way head (binary control 0.842 vs main 0.718 on FR, same data) and the both-class views cost
  reenactment. That is narrower and true.

**B3. Major — the symmetric-view variant opens a 34-point evasion route; the abstract presents it as the fix.**
Location: abstract lines 70–72; lines 151–154, 444–454; Table S (`tab_v4_evasion`) last rows.
- What a reviewer would say: "You condemn P1 smoothing-only as an evasion route (miss rise +56 to +63). Your retrained main
  model has a miss rise on whole-crop-smoothed forgeries of +28.1 / +33.8 / +34.1. Its miss rate goes from 43.5% to 77.6%
  under strong bilateral smoothing at your fixed threshold. The main text mentions only its false alarms (1.0–1.7%). The
  supplement has the number; the abstract and Sec. 4.5 do not."
- Nuance the authors should add (it helps them): the variant's AUC with both classes smoothed is 0.796 against 0.747 for
  P0 and 0.688 for the main model, so separability is better. The miss rise is a calibration shift at a threshold fixed on
  untreated frames. That is the paper's own "moves errors, not separability" finding, and it should be stated as such.
- Evidence: `tab_v4_evasion.tex` (variant +28.1/+33.8/+34.1, AUC 0.796; main −25.1/−42.3/−44.3, AUC 0.688; binary control

- Fix: Put the variant's miss rise next to its false-alarm numbers in the abstract and Sec. 4.5. Report both readings
  (fixed threshold and AUC with both classes smoothed) for every arm in a main-paper table. Untested so far, and the
  realistic attack: apply face-local scripted smoothing (`op_smooth`) and the commercial-style smoothing to Celeb-DF-v2
  forgeries. Whole-crop bilateral is not what a forger running a beauty app produces.

### C. Fairness of comparisons

**C1. Major — "on par with Effort and Forensics Adapter on DF40 face swap" is false for Effort in the Celeb-DF domain, and

### F. Novelty and citations

**F1. Major — the novelty is narrower than the contributions list states.**
- Measurement: Libourel et al. (3 detectors, video AUC), Saeed et al. (6 detectors, evasion), Concas et al. (2 CNNs, both
  errors) and LAION-Mobile (threshold calibration) cover the phenomenon. The new parts are the scale (17), the one-rule
  two-sided protocol, and the paired threshold-free statistic on a commercial service with per-operation dose response. Sell
  those.
- "Train on the corruption, become robust to it" is standard (augmentation-for-robustness literature). The class-conditional
  part, where which class receives the view sets the error sign and a class-asymmetric form becomes a label, is shortcut
  learning (Geirhos et al. 2020) and class-dependent augmentation (Balestriero, Bottou, LeCun, NeurIPS 2022). Neither is
  cited, and a reviewer will cite both against you. Position the finding as the first controlled demonstration of this in
  face-forgery detection with beautification, with the sign of the error measured.
- Fusion with a boundary detector: the Alpha Blending Hypothesis paper (already cited as alphablend2026, arXiv 2605.10334)
  itself ensembles its blending detector (BlenD) with blend-resistant models and reports 94.0% AUROC. That is direct prior art
  for Sec. 4.7's fusion and must be discussed there.
- Footprint-supervised evidence maps: joint detection and segmentation of manipulated faces is established (Nguyen et al.
  2019 multi-task; Face X-ray, which is cited only as generalisation; HiFi-Net, CVPR 2023). The restoration test is the new
  part.

## 5. Frank accept/reject estimates

**CVPR main track: reject (about 10–15% even after fixes 1–10).** CVPR reviewers will read this as a benchmark/analysis paper
wearing a method paper's clothes. Its detector is dominated by its own control, is at chance on Celeb-DF-domain reenactment,
and its best recipe is scheme-specific with a stated cost. The "mechanism" covers two colour filters. The finding "augment one
class and you teach a label" will be met with "this is shortcut learning". Measurement papers do get into CVPR, but they
need a single crisp, surprising headline and a dataset or benchmark others will use. If the authors want CVPR, the path is
to release the two-sided protocol plus the paired commercial-retouching benchmark as a benchmark contribution, with a fresh
smoothing source and the P3 cell. Even then it is borderline.

**CVPR workshop (e.g. a media-forensics or deepfake workshop): accept likely (about 65–75%)** after fixes 1, 3, 4 and 9 and
a cut to workshop length. Workshop reviewers value the 17-detector two-sided measurement, the threshold-free pair statistic,
the evasion-route finding and the honesty about negative results.

**IJCB: borderline to likely accept (about 45–55%) if reframed.** Beautification versus forgery is squarely biometric, and
IJCB reviewers care about false accusation of genuine users. That requires the biometric beautification literature (F3),
the identity-preservation operationalisation (A1), the false-accusation framing up front, and fixes 1–5. As currently written
(IEEEtran, named authors, CVPR-style claims, no biometric related work), it would be a weak reject.

**IEEE TIFS: major revision at best; as is, reject-and-resubmit (about 25–35% eventual acceptance after a full revision).**
TIFS has room for the supplement's material and values the pre-registration. It will demand: identity-cluster statistics (C10),
a deployable threshold (C6), a fresh commercial source (B11), the mechanism on published detectors (B1), seeds (B14), the
complete exposure design (B5), the operational boundary (A1), and an explanation evaluation beyond oracle restoration (B10).
Fixes 1–9 plus items E10–E16 would make it a credible TIFS paper. It is probably the best long-term home for the full body
of work.
