\section{Pre-registration and what was read when}
Every bar quoted in the paper was written into a dated file before the corresponding model was trained or the
corresponding test source was read. Celeb-DF-B and the DFD filter set were read once per model, after the checkpoint had
been chosen on validation data. The Celeb-DF-v2 filter set was used to diagnose which filters cause the damage
(Sec.~4.2 of the paper), so it is a development source for every design choice made after that point; the DFD filter set
was built afterwards from a second filter library. No per-filter result of the DFD set was inspected. The checkpoint of
every model is chosen on validation data only: FF++ validation macro-F1 (three-way, FF++ only), FF++ validation AUC
(binary), or the mean of FF++ validation macro-F1 and balanced accuracy on held-out originals and renders of the two
training services (three-way, full data). An earlier selection rule (F1 of the filter class on an imbalanced validation
set) picked the epoch with the lowest specificity on untouched originals; it was replaced before the models of this paper
were trained, and the earlier model appears only in Table~\ref{tab:cgd_all}.

\textbf{The operator.} A differentiable edit $B(x;u)$ on a face crop in $[0,1]$ with every component bounded by a
sigmoid: per-channel gamma, gain and lift; a $3\times3$ colour matrix close to the identity; saturation from monochrome
to 1.7; contrast about the mean; a $4\times4$ luminance field and a per-channel tint field, upsampled bilinearly; a blend
toward a Gaussian-smoothed copy; additive grain. Its parameters were fitted by gradient descent to 1,000 pairs each of
four measured families (Table~\ref{tab:operator}); the search of the ``measured edits'' arms starts from a fitted edit and
is projected onto the 0.5th--99.5th percentile range of the fitted parameters.

\textbf{The search works; the training does not transfer.} On 400 FF++ validation frames the reference model keeps
96.0\% of genuine faces genuine without an edit, 71.0\% under a random draw of the operator, 41.0\% under the worst of
four draws, 8.5\% under a one-step signed-gradient search and 0.0\% under ten steps; forged faces stay forged in 93.5,
89.5, 83.0, 74.0 and 20.0\% of cases. After training on the one-step worst case the genuine accuracy under that search
is 71.5\% (25.0\% under ten steps), and after the three-step variant 76.5\% (41.5\%). The gain does not carry over to the
app presets or to filters from a library the operator was not fitted to, and the forged accuracy under random edits falls
from 89.5 to 79.5\%: with a label loss, edited views of genuine faces dominate the signal and the model learns that a
heavily edited face is less likely forged.

\textbf{A learned inverse.} The exact algebraic inverse of the invertible part of the operator restores an edited frame
to 53--58\,dB when the true parameters are given. A ResNet-18 estimator trained to predict them from the edited frame
alone reaches 26.7\,dB on random edits (25.9 before) and 22.7\,dB on the held-out filters (21.0 before), and moves
untouched frames to 34.4\,dB. In front of the detector it changes the false alarms under the held-out filters from 21.5
to 23.6\%. A single image does not determine its colour transform.

\textbf{Edit sensitivity as evidence.} The response of the forged logit to six fixed probe edits is larger for genuine
than for forged frames on average, but inside the deciles of the clean logit where both classes occur the two respond
alike (1.15 vs 0.96; 0.48 vs 0.10), so the asymmetry is a ceiling effect; adding the responses to the logit leaves the
false alarms under filters at 16--22\%.

\textbf{A decision pooled from the evidence map.} With a zero map target on every beautified item and the forged logit
taken as the mean of the top 5\% map cells, the map stays dark on filtered genuine faces (mean logit $-10.2$ against
$+1.9$ for the edit map) and the false alarms under filters fall from 21.5 to 14.7\%, but the beautification-specific
miss rise on Celeb-DF-B grows from 7.5 to 14.9 points.

\textbf{Fine-tuning a released checkpoint.} The released Effort weights were loaded into our pipeline (the mapped
model reproduces the released scorer: Celeb-DF-B AUC 0.909, AUC$_b$ 0.808) and fine-tuned for two epochs. With both-class
exposure the miss rise falls from 11.2 to 2.7 points, the false alarms on beautified genuine faces rise from 4.7 to
10.3\%, AUC$_b$ stays at 0.803 and the untreated AUC falls by 0.014; without beautified views the same fine-tuning gives
5.8 points and 9.4\%. The operating point moves; separability does not.

\section{Sources that were examined and excluded}
\textbf{AR beauty filters.} On paired Instagram AR-filter images, a model trained with such pairs called 94.8\% of the
filtered images \emph{filter} with the face hull greyed out: the capture pipeline, not the face, carries the label. No
claim is made on AR filters.
\textbf{WildDeepfake.} The 800 test frames available to us come from six sequences, which gives intervals of
0.3--0.5 AUC; not reported.
\textbf{DFDC, DFDCP, DF40.} Not available to us in a form with genuine and forged frames from the same pipeline.
