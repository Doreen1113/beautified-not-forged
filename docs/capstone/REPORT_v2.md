# Beautified Is Not Forged: a three-way face detector with checkable evidence

*Capstone report, version 2 (2026-10-27). This version replaces the claims of `REPORT.md` (2026-10-15); the differences are listed in `REPORT_v2_CHANGES.md`. Every number is followed by its source file in parentheses; paths are relative to the repository root `C:\My_Project\AIGC`. Results for the two training seeds of the main model are written as "s1 / s2". Where a difference lies inside the spread between seeds, it is written as no change. The cover page and the Chinese abstract are to be regenerated from this version.*

## Abstract

Face-forgery detectors answer "real" or "fake". A genuine photograph that has only been through a beauty app or a retouching service is neither, and once the skin has been smoothed the detectors tend to call it fake. We score seventeen published detectors by one rule on pixel-aligned pairs of genuine faces and their commercially smoothed renders, and on app-beautified deepfake videos, and find errors in both directions: the smoothed genuine face scores as more forged than its own original, and the beautified deepfake is missed more often. A training experiment with the architecture, data and recipe held fixed shows that what decides the first error is whether skin smoothing was shown to both classes during training; the accusation falls from 54 to 12 percent. An edge-treatment experiment shows what the detectors react to: a face that is smoother than its own surroundings. The system built for this capstone turns a detector trained with that recipe into something a person can check. It answers real, forged or beautified, marks the face part its evidence came from, and shows a short explanation whose every sentence code composes from the model's own output and checks before display. The marked region is tested by restoring its original pixels, which removes 94 percent of the model's part-level forgery decisions, against about half for Grad-CAM. Free-form descriptions written by vision-language models were tested the same way and failed a blind matching test, which is why no model is allowed to describe the image. The third answer does not improve detection; it adds the answer "beautified", at a stated cost: some beautified deepfakes land in that class. A small pre-registered human study of whether the explanation helps people judge is in progress, with results pending.

## 1. Introduction

Many face photographs pass through a beauty app or a retouching service before anyone else sees them, and face-forgery detectors answer only real or fake. Take FFHQ face 17006: the same person, nothing forged, processed by a commercial retouching service's strongest skin smoothing (Smoothing-90). The forgery score of SBI rises from 0.711 to 0.999, that of DFD-HR from 0.426 to 0.936 and that of Forensics Adapter from 0.104 to 0.844, and each crosses the threshold at which that detector accuses five percent of untouched originals (`docs/demo_ru/cases/cases.json`, case `A_17006`, drawn by `docs/demo_ru/build_cases.py` with seed 20261022, not chosen by hand). This is not one unlucky face. On 2,210 pixel-aligned pairs of originals and smoothed renders, sixteen of seventeen published detectors score the smoothed face above its own original in most pairs, fifteen of them in 89 to 99 percent of pairs (`results/research/alipair_zeroshot_20260929/main_claim_A.json`; wording calibrated in `docs/capstone/STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §6, claim A1).

Three things are missing from the usual answer. First, a binary detector has no "beautified" output, so a smoothed genuine face is either accused or passed as untouched. Second, the usual answer to "where is the evidence" is a post-hoc heat map; on the same backbone, restoring the original pixels inside the region that Grad-CAM marks removes only about half of the part-level forgery decisions, not far above a fixed region in the middle of the face (`docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_cgd_explain_all.tex`). Third, the usual answer to "why" is a vision-language model describing the image; in a blind test, such descriptions are matched to their own photograph about as often as a coin flip (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §2 and §5), while the text-similarity metrics those methods report rank a constant sentence above a fine-tuned describer (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/MATCHING_v4_LORA.md`, §8).

A person who is handed a verdict about a face needs three things: which of real, forged or beautified it is; where on the face the evidence lies; and whether what the system says matches what it computed. The setting imposes three constraints. There is one photograph and no original to compare with. The photograph must not leave the device. And nothing may be shown that code cannot match to the model's output. The goal is therefore a system that, given one still face photograph, gives one of three answers, marks the evidence region, shows only sentences that have been checked, and is then tested on people to see whether it helps them judge.

Each missing piece has a trap. The third answer can become a hiding place for forgeries: with the same architecture, showing smoothing only to genuine faces during training teaches "smooth skin means genuine", and heavily smoothed forgeries are then missed 56 to 63 points more often (`docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_v4_tradeoff.tex`, row "P1 smoothing only"). The marked region has to be the model's actual reason, and heat maps are barely better than guessing the middle of the face. And a language model that words the sentences makes errors the rules do not see: while the demo was being recorded, the sentence "The model's decision is fake, with a score of 1.00 concentrated on the mouth" passed the check in force at the time, and the two rules written afterwards blocked 390 English and 206 Chinese texts that had been shipped on the holdout (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/nl_v5/GATE_V3_RESULTS.notes.md`).

One module answers each challenge. The detector, a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 with LoRA shared with the laboratory paper, outputs real, forged or beautified and is trained with skin smoothing shown to genuine and forged faces alike. In a controlled comparison on FF++ data only, that choice takes the accusation of commercially smoothed genuine faces from 54.2 / 56.1 to 11.7 percent, with no change on beautified deepfake videos (`results/research/alipair_zeroshot_20260929/smoothing90_thresholdfree.json`, rows "P0 s1", "P0 s2", "P2 s1"; `docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_v4_tradeoff.tex`). On face 17006 the system says "real" for the original and "beautified" for the render, with forgery scores 0.003 and 0.001 (`cases.json`). An evidence head trained on the exact footprint of every edit outputs a 16 by 16 map, 478 facial landmarks turn the map into a part name (eyes, nose, mouth, skin, contour or the whole face), and a restoration test validates the name. Code composes the sentences from the model's record and a rule checker matches each sentence back to the record before it is shown. A language-model rewording was tested and removed: once the numbers and the calibration sentence had been moved to code, its wording collapsed to about twenty strings per style and differed from the template in nothing that was measured (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/nl_v5/P3_RESULTS.notes.md`). The whole path runs in the browser and the photograph is not uploaded (`docs/demo_ru/README.md`).

The contributions are, in the order of the sections:

1. A face checker that gives three answers, marks its evidence on the face and shows only checked sentences, running in the browser (Section 2).
2. One test that could fail for each promise: the controlled with-and-without comparison for the answer (Section 4), the restoration test for the region, and the checker's catches for the sentences (Section 5).
3. Evidence that free-form descriptions of the image are unreliable, which is the reason no model is allowed to describe it (Section 5).
4. A pre-registered human study of whether the explanation helps people judge (Section 6); its result is pending.

The audit of seventeen published detectors (Section 3) is the reason a third answer is needed. It is not a leaderboard, and this report claims no ranking over any published detector.

**Division of labour.** This capstone and the laboratory's paper share three things: the detector itself (the CLIP ViT-L/14 + LoRA three-way model and its evidence head), the measurement of the seventeen published detectors, and the training comparison with and without beautified views; the report cites them and gives the result file next to each number. What the capstone built is the part that turns the detector's output into something a person can see and check: part naming from facial landmarks, the sentence composer and the rule checker, the tests of language-model rewording and of free-form vision-language descriptions, the browser system with cases whose answers are known, and the pre-registered human study. The capstone's claims fall only on the latter (answer, place, sentences, people); it claims no detector ranking, and the paper's mechanism analysis is not counted as a capstone contribution. (Script-by-script attribution starts from `docs/capstone/JUDGE_REHEARSAL_20261022.md`, Section 2, and is to be confirmed by each member before submission.)

## 2. What the system does

Figure 2 (`docs/paper_v2/figs/fig_method_v8b.png`, panel b) draws the detector; Figure 3 (`docs/capstone/pipeline_en.png`) draws the full path but still contains the language-model stage and must be redrawn (see "Figures").

**Three answers.** The classes are defined by what was changed, not by which tool changed it (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, Section 2). A face is *forged* when the person, expression, action or voice shown was not captured: an identity swap, a reenactment, a synthesised face, or a replaced part. It is *beautified* when, after capture, only colour, skin tone, skin texture or the shape of the person's own features changed, while identity, expression and pose stay as captured. A beautified forgery is forged. MediaPipe's 478 landmarks locate and crop the face on the user's device, with the same crop as in training (`docs/demo_ru/README.md`). The main detector is a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 with rank-16 LoRA on the attention projections of all 24 blocks, 3.2 M of 306 M parameters trained (`docs/paper_v2/FIGURES_V8B.md`, Fig. 2 facts; `results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/train_ru_clip.py`). Two linear heads on the pooled token give a forged logit f and a beautified logit b, combined as P(fake) = f, P(beautified) = (1 − f) b and P(real) = (1 − f)(1 − b), so the forgery decision reads f only and does not depend on the beautified logit (`train_ru5.py`, `RUCLIP5.forward`). A third head scores four beautification operations (smoothing, whitening, eye enlarging, face slimming) at a threshold of 0.5; only the smoothing score is reliable (Section 5.4).

**Where the evidence is.** A linear map from the 256 patch tokens gives a 16 by 16 evidence map, trained against the exact footprint of each edit. The classifier does not read the map, so whether the map explains the decision has to be tested (Section 5.1). Code builds masks for eyes, nose, mouth, skin and contour from the landmarks and names the part with the highest mean evidence; when eyes, nose and mouth all reach 0.5 it names the whole face (rule B, `docs/demo_ru/README.md`).

**Sentences composed and checked by code.** The decision, the three probabilities, the four operation scores, the mean evidence per part and the named part form one record. From that record code writes the explanation: the decision with its score; the region; for a beautified decision, the operations at or above the threshold, or a sentence that none reaches it; and a fixed sentence that the score is the model's raw output and not a calibrated probability (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/explain_v2.py`; browser port `docs/demo_ru/explain_t2.js`). Before anything is shown, a rule checker matches the text to the record on decision, region, operations, numbers, forbidden phrases and sentence count (H1 to H6) and rejects a decision score compared with the operation threshold (G7), any wording about certainty, negated forms included (G8), and a decision score attached to a face part (G9) (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/validate_text.py`; `nl_v5/gate_v3.py`; rule table in `docs/capstone/REPORT.md`, Appendix B). Four questions the record cannot answer (comparison with the original, identity, certainty, the meaning of the score) get fixed sentences. A server mode that rewords the sentences with a language model remains in the page for testing and is not part of the evaluated system (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, item D01).

**In the browser.** ONNX Runtime Web runs the model on WebAssembly, and the contour, the part name and the sentences are computed on the device. The default browser model is an EfficientNet-B4 with the same evidence head (66.97 MB, 17.6 M parameters; wall time median 559 ms per photograph in headless Chrome on one WebAssembly thread); a RepViT evidence model serves older phones (18.11 MB, 4.72 M; 176 ms) (`docs/demo_ru/README.md`, "Models"). Both are smaller than the CLIP main model of Sections 3 to 5, and the default one agrees with it on 43 of 50 decisions and 38 of 50 named parts (`docs/demo_ru/README.md`, ONNX export section). The page ships cases with known answers drawn by a fixed seed (`docs/demo_ru/cases.html`) rather than inviting live selfies, for the reason given next.

**What it does not do.** It does not name beautification operations other than smoothing. On the retouching service's single-operation renders, the operation head finds smoothing with a true-positive rate of 0.943 but eye enlarging, face slimming and whitening at only 0.196, 0.322 and 0.375 (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/READOUT46.md`, `presence.eq4.TP`), and on Instagram AR filters its recall is 0.003, 0.03, 0.18 and 0.24 for eye enlarging, slimming, whitening and smoothing (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/FB_OPS.md`). It does not cover Instagram AR filters: the available pairs (FairBeauty, B-LFW) did not pass their originals through the same rendering pipeline as the filtered versions, and with the face greyed out the multi-operation arm still calls 100 percent of the originals and 99.5 percent of the renders fake, so filter and pipeline cannot be told apart (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-22 16:40). It is not a live-selfie demo: untouched originals of the retouching service are called beautified by 20.8 / 19.6 percent of the main model (`main_claim_A.json`, `native_orig`), 26 of 100 untouched FF++ faces on the holdout are called beautified and 10 forged (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §1), and ordinary FairFace photographs are called real by only 23.2 percent (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/PRE_DECLARED.md`, Addendum 58, diagnosis). It is built for still photographs of swap and part-level forgeries; on DF40 reenactment it is at chance in the Celeb-DF domain (Section 4.5).

## 3. Why detectors confuse beautification with forgery

Every comparison with a published detector below is an audit of the problem, run with released weights under one rule. Our own model saw more training data than any of them, so its rows are a reference, not a contest (`docs/capstone/STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §6).

### 3.1 Seventeen detectors, two directions

**Direction A, the accusation.** The test set is 2,210 FFHQ originals and their renders by the Alibaba retouching service (RetouchingFFHQ [17]), aligned pixel for pixel, so the only difference inside a pair is the retouching. The threshold-free reading asks in what share of pairs the render scores as more forged than its own original (50 percent means no effect); the thresholded reading fixes, per detector, the threshold that accuses 5 percent of the untouched originals and counts the renders above it. Table 1 gives both for the strongest smoothing (Smoothing-90). Sixteen of seventeen detectors score the smoothed face above its own original in most pairs, fifteen in 89 to 99 percent of pairs and CADDM in 61 percent; NPR, whose scores saturate at 1.0 on these stills, does not. At the threshold, thirteen of seventeen call 36 to 69 percent of the smoothed genuine faces fake (`main_claim_A.json`; `STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §3). The effect is specific to smoothing: the service's whitening, face slimming and eye enlarging at the same strength are accused at about the nominal 5 percent (`STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §3.3). A second, open-source beauty engine reproduces the accusation for the published detectors: GPUPixel's skin smoothing at camera scale, applied to genuine Celeb-DF-v2 frames, is accused above 15 percent by 14 of 15 face detectors at its strongest level, against 2.4 to 4.9 percent for a resize-only null form (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/GPX38.md`). Our main model, scored on the same frames on 2026-10-29, accuses 13.7 / 13.1 percent at the strongest level with an AUC of 0.80 / 0.82 when both classes are smoothed; the only face detectors below it, UnivFD and SRM (12.4 and 12.5 percent), keep an AUC of 0.62 and 0.66. Its cost is on the other side: at the untreated threshold it misses 45.8 / 41.3 percent of the smoothed forgeries (`GPX38.md`, rows `clipe5_s20260929`, `clipe5_s20260930`).

**Direction B, the escape.** Celeb-DF-B [14] holds 232 genuine and 232 deepfake Celeb-DF-v2 videos, each untreated, beautified by an app preset, and re-encoded without any filter, so the effect of beautification alone is the rise in the miss rate under the preset minus the rise under re-encoding alone. Net of re-encoding, app beautification raises the miss rate of 14 of the 17 detectors, by 3 to 22 points (Effort +11.0 [7.4, 14.5], Forensics Adapter +15.2, SBI +21.8; DFD-HR +3.6 and EfficientNet-B4 +3.4 are the smallest; UnivFD falls, NPR and CADDM do not change) (`results/research/celebdfb_v2_20260927/fair_cdfb.json`; `docs/capstone/MAIN_CLAIMS_20261022.md`, Direction B). The same presets barely raise the accusation of beautified genuine frames (median absolute change 1.6 points; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-22 16:40), so the app presets err in the escape direction, and the retouching service in the accusation direction.

The accusation is what FF++ training gives by default: with no beautified view, 43.5 to 56.1 percent of the smoothed renders are accused, and showing smoothing to forged faces only raises it to 63.8 percent. Removing smoothing from otherwise rich beautified views leaves it at 55.3 and 67.1 percent, so no other view substitutes for it. Smoothing shown to genuine faces only brings the accusation to about 1 percent and creates the opposite error, a miss rise of 56 to 63 points on smoothed forgeries. Shown to both classes it lowers the accusation to single digits, and the sign of the remaining error depends on the form: the plain P2 arms accuse smoothed faces of both classes, while the arm that applies the same smoothing forms to both classes misses 14.6 and 17.5 points more smoothed forgeries. The cue does not come from the texture-poor forgery families of FF++: an arm trained without Deepfakes and NeuralTextures accuses face-local smoothing as much as P0 (95.5 and 100 percent against 95.8 and 100 percent), so that explanation is withdrawn (`FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-23, Addendum 49).

### 3.3 The mechanism: a face smoother than its context

What do the detectors react to? With the same bilateral strength inside the face hull and only the edge treatment changed, smoothing only a ring at the hull edge is accused by a median of about 12 percent (7 to 50 percent) against 92 to 100 percent for the full face-local form, for 13 of 15 face detectors; the exceptions are the boundary and landmark detectors CADDM (94 percent on the ring) and LAA-Net (65 percent). Smoothing the whole crop, face and background alike, is accused much less by the CLIP detectors (UCF 13.6, Effort 30.3, Forensics Adapter 59.3 percent against 98 to 100 face-local) (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/BDRY38.md`; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-23, Addendum 57). The pre-declared "boundary line" explanation therefore fails, and the reading we use is that the accused pattern is a face smoother than its own context: the smoothed interior is necessary and a matching context removes much of the effect. That is the face-versus-context inconsistency which face-swap detectors are built to find and which face-local beautification reproduces by construction. The reading has partial support only: F3Net, CORE, RECCE and LAA-Net are accused on the whole crop too (92 to 99 percent), and our own main model runs the other way (81.4 percent on the whole crop against 44.5 face-local), so its low accusation on the retouching service does not extend to every form of smoothing (`BDRY38.md`; `STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §5.2).

### 3.4 What cannot fix it

Four repairs that leave training alone were measured, and each moves the error instead of removing it. *A threshold.* Under scripted smoothing of Celeb-DF-v2 frames the scores of smoothed genuine and smoothed forged faces coincide; re-setting each detector's threshold at 5 percent false alarms on smoothed genuine faces leaves the published detectors missing 67 to 100 percent of the smoothed forgeries (40 to 94 percent already untreated) and our models 52 to 77 percent (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/RECAL_MISS.md`; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-21). *Routing.* Sending smoothed inputs to an expert trained only on smoothed copies clears the accusation (51.2 to 2.1 and 79.9 to 1.3 percent at two face-local strengths; 6.5 to 0.2 percent on the commercial renders) and raises the miss rate by 30 to 46 points at the strong forms, because the expert separates smoothed faces worse than the untreated detector (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/SCE_s2.md`; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-22, Addendum 48). *Consistency training.* A smoothing-invariant consistency penalty removes the accusation of face-local smoothing, but at a threshold re-set on smoothed genuine faces it is not five points better than the view-only control, so its gain is the operating point and it is reported as a recalibration-equivalent trade-off (`FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-21, RECAL_MISS entry; `docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_sic.tex`). *Test-time context matching.* Since the cue is a face smoother than its context (Section 3.3), a zero-training rule scored each image together with a whole-crop-smoothed copy and called it forged only if both exceeded their thresholds. It clears the accusation (10 of 15 face detectors below 30 percent on face-locally smoothed genuine frames), but at a matched 5 percent false-alarm rate its miss rate is within a few points of the re-set threshold for every detector (0 of 15 better by 10 points), and the second gate raises the miss rate on untreated forgeries by 20 to 35 points (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/CTX38.md`; `PRE_DECLARED.md`, Addendum 61, pre-registered on 2026-10-27 and read out the same evening). This is why the system's answer is a training recipe and a third output, with the recipe's costs stated in Section 4.5.

## 4. How the system is trained

### 4.1 Recipe

Each training step draws one genuine, one forged and one beautified item from one genuine frame. The pool holds 41,512 genuine FF++ (c23) frames and 7,015 FFHQ originals (816 more are kept for checkpoint selection); 41,526 FF++ forgeries, 61,510 part-level edits with exact footprints, and self-blends built online; and, for the beautified class, 18,734 renders of two commercial retouching services (Tencent 6,924, Megvii 11,810), their 50,112 single-operation components, scripted operations and 14 colour filters on the FF++ frames, and colour grading online. The forged item is beautified on top about half of the time, so smoothing is seen on both classes (`docs/paper_v2/FIGURES_V8B.md`, Fig. 2 facts; `results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/train_ru5.py`). The third service, Alibaba, is never used in training; it was read repeatedly during the study, so it is an analysis set rather than a blind test (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, item R14). Two training seeds (20260929, 20260930) are reported throughout, because the difference between them exceeds the sampling interval of either (`STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §5.5).

### 4.2 The fair comparison

The only fair comparison is our own architecture with and without beautified views, on the same public data, with nothing from the retouching service: arms P0 and P2 of Table 2. With the architecture, data and recipe fixed, giving beautified views to both classes lowers the accusation of the commercially smoothed genuine faces from 54.2 / 56.1 to 11.7 percent (`smoothing90_thresholdfree.json`, rows "P0 s1", "P0 s2", "P2 s1"), and from 43.5 to 56.1 percent to 6.9 to 11.7 percent over three seeds (`tab_v4_tradeoff.tex`). On beautified deepfake videos the change is zero: the AUC after beautification is 0.860 / 0.883 / 0.883 without the views and 0.879 / 0.893 / 0.877 with them, inside the seed spread, and the beautification-only miss rise moves by −7.2, −4.0 and +2.0 points over the three seeds, with no stable sign (`tab_v4_tradeoff.tex`, Celeb-DF-B columns). The price is paid elsewhere: untreated Celeb-DF-v2 video AUC falls from 0.938 to 0.940 to 0.917 to 0.935, and DF40 reenactment in the FF++ domain from 0.906 / 0.877 to 0.809 / 0.785 (Table 2).

The full-data three-way model keeps these properties (Table 1, last two rows). Its three-way decision calls 98.8 / 99.2 percent of the smoothed renders beautified and 0.8 / 0.6 percent forged, and 78.4 / 79.8 percent of the untouched originals real (`main_claim_A.json`, `native_s90`, `native_orig`). A forgery-head-only model trained on the same data accuses 9.1 / 6.5 percent of the smoothed renders at the threshold, so the third class is not what removes the accusation; what it adds is the answer itself, visible in the paired reading, where the forgery score rises for 32.5 percent of pairs instead of 70.5 (`smoothing90_thresholdfree.json`, rows "main 3-way full", "binary full"). An earlier table typed the forgery-head-only cell as 40.6 percent; that value has no source and was corrected to 9.1 (`FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-23, correction).

### 4.3 Does the recipe transfer?

The same with-and-without comparison was run under the tuning scheme of Effort, the published CLIP detector whose fine-tuning differs from LoRA. Without beautified views that scheme accuses 56.9 / 63.2 percent of the smoothed renders; with the symmetric smoothing view it accuses 14.5 percent, although the render is still ranked above its own original in 88.4 percent of pairs. Fine-tuning the released Effort checkpoint itself for two epochs lowers the accusation only from 55.3 to 39.9 percent (`tab_v4_tradeoff.tex`, "Effort scheme" and "released Effort fine-tuned" rows; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-27). The recipe lowers the accusation under every tuning scheme tested, least for the short fine-tune of a released checkpoint.

### 4.4 Ordinary photographs

The main model recognises the retouching service's originals and FF++ frames as genuine, but calls ordinary FairFace photographs real only 23.2 percent of the time (`PRE_DECLARED.md`, Addendum 58). An arm trained with the same recipe plus FairFace training originals as additional genuine bases, with no AR render, calls FairFace validation originals real 95.9 / 96.0 percent of the time with no guardrail cost: Celeb-DF-B beautification-only miss rise +8.6 / +3.6 points against +6.7, Celeb-DF-v2 frame AUC 0.897 / 0.884 against 0.891 / 0.887, DFD 0.950 / 0.954 against 0.956 / 0.954, FF++ false alarms 5.8 / 5.2 percent (`FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-27, Addendum 58; `results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/RESULTS_clipe7W_s20260929.md`, `_s20260930.md`). It does not touch the AR-filter problem (FairBeauty renders accused 99.3 / 98.6 percent, control 75.1), as expected from a source declared confounded before the arm was run, so by its pre-declared rule it fails as an AR-filter fix. As the model for ordinary photographs it is the first arm to pass the in-the-wild test without a guardrail cost; whether to ship it is a product decision, not a claim, and every number in this report is from the main model unless stated.

### 4.5 Costs, stated

Three costs are real and are shown next to the answer wherever the system presents it. *Reenactment.* The three-way model is behind every CLIP detector on DF40 reenactment: 0.718 / 0.780 in the FF++ domain and 0.502 / 0.559 in the Celeb-DF domain, where 0.5 is chance; the forgery-head-only model on the same data reaches 0.842 and 0.647 (`docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_v4_df40.tex`). *Untouched faces called beautified.* The retouching service's originals are called beautified by 20.8 / 19.6 percent of the main model, and by 16.4 / 21.0 percent of the in-the-wild arm, above its pre-declared bar of 10 percent (`main_claim_A.json`; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-27); on the service's full set of operations the three-way output separates retouched from untouched faces at 73.45 percent balanced accuracy, below the pre-registered 75 (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/RESULTS_clipe5_s20260929.md`). *The escape route.* The beautified class is itself a place for forgeries to hide. On Celeb-DF-B the three-way decision sends 16.6 / 16.1 percent of the beautified deepfakes to "beautified", against 3.6 / 3.9 percent of the same deepfakes untreated and 5.6 / 6.3 percent after re-encoding alone; net of re-encoding, beautification moves 10.9 / 9.8 points of forgeries into that class, and they count as misses in Table 1 (`fair_cdfb.json`, `RU-clipe5` and `RU-clipe5-s2`, `native.synthesis*`; `STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §4.5). The same native decision calls 16.0 / 19.3 percent of the untreated Celeb-DF-B genuine frames fake (`fair_cdfb.json`, `native.real`). The result page therefore shows the forgery score next to every "beautified" verdict, together with this rate.

## 5. Evidence and explanations

### 5.1 Is the marked region the reason? The restoration test

For a detected part-level forgery with a known footprint, the cells of the evidence map with the highest values, of the same area as the footprint, are restored to the original pixels and the model is asked again; if the decision leaves "fake", the region was sufficient for it. The ceiling is the same test with the true footprint, and the complement test restores everything outside the region instead. The test set is held-out part-level forgeries built from FF++ test frames with test-video donors under three editors, one of which (SDXL inpainting) was never used in training (`docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_cgd_explain_all.tex`).

| Map | IoU with the true footprint (7×7) | Flip after restoring the region (%) | Ceiling, true footprint (%) | Flip after restoring everything else (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Centre prior (random region in the last column) | 0.310 / 0.249 / 0.255 | 38.6 / 40.9 / 40.2 | | 17.0 / 17.9 / 17.7 |
| Grad-CAM [19], same backbone, no head | 0.367 / 0.259 / 0.269 | 50.9 / 45.4 / 48.0 | 85.8 / 85.2 / 85.9 | 15.2 / 25.0 / 23.6 |
| Grad-CAM++ [20] | 0.414 / 0.327 / 0.330 | 51.7 / 47.8 / 48.8 | 85.8 / 85.2 / 85.9 | 7.2 / 16.7 / 19.1 |
| Score-CAM [21] | 0.408 / 0.334 / 0.341 | 56.5 / 52.4 / 52.4 | 85.8 / 85.2 / 85.9 | 5.9 / 15.4 / 17.1 |
| Ours, evidence head | 0.834 / 0.841 / 0.831 | 94.0 / 93.6 / 93.5 | 94.1 / 93.7 / 93.6 | 0.1 / 0.4 / 0.3 |

*Table 3. Restoration test on held-out part-level forgeries, donor transplant / SD-1.5 inpaint / SDXL inpaint (`docs/paper_v2/tables/tab_cgd_explain_all.tex`).*

Restoring the region the evidence head marks removes 93.5 to 94.0 percent of the detections, within 0.1 points of restoring the true footprint, and restoring everything else removes at most 0.4 percent, so the region is sufficient and necessary for the model's own decision. Heat maps on the same backbone without the head remove 45 to 57 percent, not far above a fixed centre region. The text names a part rather than a map, so the test was repeated on the named part: on the 82 correctly detected part-level forgeries of the 361-image holdout, restoring the named part removes 85.4 percent of detections against 87.8 percent for the map (difference −2.4 points [−5.6, 0.0], within the pre-declared 3 points) and 13.9 percent for a random other part. For beautified faces the evidence is weaker: restoring the named part lowers the beautified score by 0.370 on average, a random other part by 0.210 and the whole crop by 0.434 (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §3). For whole-face swaps no method finds a small sufficient region, and the text then says that the evidence is spread over the whole face.

### 5.2 Sentences composed and checked by code

All rules were developed on a 429-image set; the numbers below come from a separate holdout of 361 images (FF++ real 100, FF++ fake 40, Alibaba renders 72, part-level forgeries 99, scripted FFHQ edits 50) with no overlap in video, FFHQ index or file (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, header). The holdout was read five times while the check rules and the prompts of the later-removed language-model path were revised, so its final numbers come from a test set that informed the design and are not a blind result (`docs/capstone/REPORT.md`, §9, item 5). On all 361 records the code-composed sentences fail none of the six rules H1 to H6, which is expected by construction, since the composer and the checker read the same record (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §1). No shipped text fails the checker on re-check in any of the recorded runs (`docs/capstone/REPORT.md`, Tables 5 and 5b).

The checker's value showed on the path that was removed. A language model (Qwen2.5-7B) was allowed to reword the facts; its texts passed the six rules and still said things the record did not support, such as that the score "does not indicate how certain the model is" or a decision score "concentrated on the mouth". Two rules written down with unit tests before anything was re-run (G8, G9) blocked 390 English and 206 Chinese texts that had been shipped on the holdout; every one was read, and all but 40 Chinese false blocks were of the two intended kinds (`GATE_V3_RESULTS.notes.md`). Moving every number and the calibration sentence into code (design p3) brought the fallback rate to zero in English and met 11 of 12 pre-declared bars in each language, and left the model's own words with about 20 distinct strings per style over the 361 records (`P3_RESULTS.notes.md`; `docs/capstone/REPORT.md`, Table 5b). A rewording that cannot be told from the template in anything measured is not worth a GPU server, so the shipped system composes the sentences in code and the rewording survives only as a testing mode (`docs/capstone/MAIN_CLAIMS_20261022.md`, "no longer claimed").

### 5.3 Why no model describes the image

Before the record-based design, vision-language models were asked to add one sentence describing what the named region looks like. To test whether such a sentence carries information about the image, a judge model sees an edited face and two sentences, one written for that face and one for its untouched original, and picks the matching one (blind matching, M1); an image-specific writer should be matched well above 50 percent, and the pre-declared reading calls an interval that contains 50 "filler". A positive control with two different people is matched at 95.3 to 99.3 percent by every judge (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/report/REPORT_DRAFT.md`, §5.2).

| Writer | Set | Blind matching M1 [95 % CI] | Pairs with identical sentences | BLEU-4 / CIDEr-D against reference sentences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-VL-7B, prompt v3 | holdout, 162 pairs | 52.8 [49.4, 56.2] | 36.4 % | 6.8 / 0.186 |
| Qwen2.5-VL-7B + LoRA (20,000 code-labelled sentences) | holdout | 51.3 [49.3, 53.2] | 74.5 % | 38.9 / 4.196 |
| FakeVLM, released weights, end to end | development, 177 pairs | 50.8 [48.3, 53.2] | 37.3 % | |
| Constant sentence (the part's "normal range" sentence) | | not applicable | | 44.4 / 4.322 |

*Table 4. Free-form descriptions (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §2 row F and §5; `results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/HOLDOUT_V4_LORA.md`; `MATCHING_v4_LORA.md`, §8; text similarity on 1,150 held-out code-labelled samples).*

No writer reaches the image-specific bar. The untuned writer gave the same sentence to the edited face and its original in 102 of 177 development pairs (`results/research/explain_vlm_20261012/MATCHING_v2.md`, §6). Fine-tuning raised BLEU-4 from 6.8 to 38.9 and CIDEr-D from 0.186 to 4.196, and a writer that outputs one fixed sentence per part scores higher on both with no image information by construction; text similarity measures agreement with the reference distribution and cannot tell a describer from a constant. FakeVLM, which decides and explains end to end, is wrong on 37.7 percent [32.0, 43.4] of the real and fake crops, and its most common paragraph begins "This is a real image. The person exhibits natural skin textures" for edited and original faces alike (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §5). A model sentence would also raise the time per image from 0.037 s to 0.74 to 1.08 s and the GPU memory peak from 1.17 GB to 17.82 GB (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §4). This is why the final system lets no model describe the image.

### 5.4 Which operations can be named

The system names smoothing and nothing else, and the reason is measured rather than assumed. Within one vendor, a dedicated EfficientNet-B0 trained on a single operation at the service's strongest level, on identity-disjoint faces, separates renders from originals with an AUROC of 0.933 [0.922, 0.943] for eye enlarging, 0.948 for face slimming, 0.980 [0.973, 0.986] for whitening and 0.9995 for smoothing; a cross-operation matrix shows the whitening and eye classifiers to be specific to their operation, while the slimming classifier also fires on smoothing renders (0.797) (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/ORACLE_OPS.md`). Those signals do not transfer across vendors: the same classifiers trained on the Megvii and Tencent components and tested on Alibaba reach 0.690 for whitening, 0.722 for eye enlarging and 0.703 for slimming at the strongest level, against a bar of 0.85, while smoothing transfers at 0.999 (`results/research/retouch_unified_20260929/XVENDOR_OPS.md`; `FINDINGS.md`, 2026-10-27, Addendum 60). Within a vendor, whitening and eye enlarging leave a trace that our general operation head does not find; across vendors, only smoothing does. The page therefore shows the four operation scores but states that only the smoothing name is supported; one human-study item (an Alibaba face-slimming render) carries a sentence naming smoothing, which Section 6 discloses.

## 6. Human study

### 6.1 Design

The study asks whether the system's checked explanation changes what a person decides about a face, compared with the verdict alone and with a free-form FakeVLM description. It was pre-declared before any participant and amended five times before any counted participant; nothing in the declaration changes after the first counted participant (`docs/capstone/human_study/PRE_DECLARED_HUMAN_STUDY.md`). It is within-subject: 12 face crops from the development set of the explanation study, never the holdout (4 genuine, 4 forged, 4 beautified; genuine and beautified items each mix two sources; exactly two items the model got wrong), and three conditions rotated by participant index so that every participant sees 4 trials per condition and every item appears in every condition once in every three consecutive participants. C0 shows the verdict and score; C1 adds the evidence contour on the named part, next to the original, and the code-composed sentences in Traditional Chinese; C2 adds a literal Traditional Chinese translation of FakeVLM's own description with its verdict sentence removed, so that the conditions differ in the explanation and not in the verdict or the language. Per trial the participant answers real, forged or beautified, gives a confidence and, for forged or beautified, names the changed part; usefulness and trust items for C1 and C2 and the SUS for C1 follow. The primary endpoint is three-way accuracy per condition; H1 (C1 above C0) is tested with a one-sided paired t test over participants, with +10 points as the smallest effect of interest rather than a pass bar, and the pre-declared paired bootstrap interval is reported beside it. Over-trust on the two model-wrong items is descriptive; part identification and SUS are exploratory (Amendments 3 and 5).

The study is small. With 24 participants and 12 images it detects a difference of about 18 to 19 points at 80 percent power (16.5 in one scenario), so a true +10 effect would be found with a probability of only about 0.3 to 0.4; an interval that contains zero will be reported as "not determined", with the effects it does not exclude, and never as "no effect" (Amendment 5, item i). Screens of the three conditions are in `docs/capstone/human_study/screens/` (`study_desktop_trial_C0.png`, `_C1.png`, `_C2.png`). Two disclosures are fixed in advance: item `alibaba:FaceLifting_90:17521` (face slimming) carries the C1 sentence naming smoothing at 0.93, which is the wrong operation, so part identification will be reported with and without it; and the participant-facing definition of "forged" does not mention reenactment although one item is a NeuralTextures frame (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, items H01 and H02).

### 6.2 Pilot

Before Amendment 5, 13 acquaintances of the authors (one submitted twice) ran an older page with 24 items, a 6/3/3 split per participant and a source confound (all genuine items were FF++ news frames and all beautified items sharp FFHQ photographs). Pooled accuracy was C0 35/57 = 61 percent, C1 33/54 = 61 percent and C2 33/57 = 58 percent (`docs/capstone/human_study/pilot/PILOT_20261028.md`, from the `trials` tab of the collector sheet; the per-participant means are 65, 64 and 60 percent). On the items the model itself got wrong, accuracy fell from 4/10 with the image alone to 1/8 with the composed sentences and 2/10 with the free-text explanation, while on the items it got right it was 31/47, 32/46 and 31/47: the explanations did not raise accuracy and, on these few trials, participants followed the model into its errors. The counts are too small for a test and are reported as they are. These are pilot numbers from a design that has since changed; they are excluded from the counted study by Amendment 5, were not used for any design choice, and show descriptively no sign that any explanation changed accuracy.

### 6.3 Status

The study went live on 2026-10-27 (CUTOFF_UTC 2026-10-27T11:40:33Z, recorded in the declaration's go-live note). The existing collector is kept and pilot rows are excluded by a version filter and by the cutoff; consent is given on the page with neutral defaults; the advisor was not consulted about ethics review before the first counted participant, a decision of the student that the declaration records; whether the 12 FakeVLM translations were checked by a person outside the study is not recorded. The analysis script (`docs/capstone/human_study/analyse.py`) was tested on a synthetic export (24 valid sessions counted; pilot, invalid and duplicate rows excluded with their reasons). No result exists at the time of writing. The result, whatever it is, replaces this paragraph; the two sentences prepared for it are in `docs/capstone/STORYLINE_20261023.md`, Section 3, contribution 4.

## 7. Limitations and what we no longer claim

**Claims withdrawn or weakened since `REPORT.md`.** The list below follows `docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md` and `docs/capstone/STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`; the full list with reasons is in `REPORT_v2_CHANGES.md`.

- The three-way model is not "the only model low on both axes" and does not "handle both directions": CADDM is lower on both raw axes, and the forgery-head-only model on the same data is as low on the accusation and higher in AUC after beautification. The defensible sentence is that no published detector beats ours on both axes, read as an audit (`STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §6, C1 and C2).
- The third class adds the answer "beautified"; it does not improve detection and costs reenactment accuracy (`tab_v4_df40.tex`).
- Language-model rewording is not part of the system (Section 5.2); the system names no operation other than smoothing (Section 5.4) and claims nothing about Instagram AR filters (Section 2).
- Symmetric smoothing views lower the accusation but do not "remove both errors" (Table 2), and "detectors that read smooth skin detect reenactment better" is withdrawn, the correlation across published detectors not being significant (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, item R04).
- The cue is neither the texture-poor FF++ families nor the blending boundary (Sections 3.2 and 3.3).
- No prevalence of beautification among genuine photographs is claimed, the project having no source for one.
- The escape route is 10.9 / 9.8 points net of re-encoding, not "about 12.5"; Effort's miss rise under the app presets is +11.0 points net of re-encoding, not 27.8 to 51.4 percent (`STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, items 3 and 4).
- The Alibaba source is an analysis set, not a held-out test, and the two audit test sets were read many times during the study.

**Limitations that remain.**

1. *Sources.* Direction A rests on one commercial service and FFHQ portraits; the open-source engine reproduces the accusation for the published detectors but the main model was not scored on it. Direction B rests on 232 videos and four presets of one dataset, whose genuine identities overlap with the training data (`external_data/celebdfb/Celeb-DF-B/PROVENANCE.md`).
2. *Seeds.* Two training seeds differ by more than their sampling intervals (paired reading −8.8 points, accusation −3.9 points, AUC −0.017 between seeds), so training variance is not estimated and every number is reported for both (`STAT_AUDIT_20261023.md`, §5.5).
3. *The main model's own smoothing shortcut.* Its training code gives genuine items face-local smoothing and forged items without landmarks a whole-crop filter, and the model calls 81.4 percent of whole-crop-smoothed genuine frames forged (`BDRY38.md`); a retrained symmetric-view variant removes this on both seeds but fails its pre-declared replacement rule on both, so it stays a variant (`docs/CONSISTENCY_AUDIT_20261023.md`, item R12).
4. *Untouched photographs called beautified and reenactment at chance* (Section 4.5).
5. *The checker is rule-based.* It guarantees agreement with the record on decision, region, operations and numbers and the absence of certainty wording, nothing outside those categories, and its word lists cannot read meaning: it blocks the correct Chinese sentence 「無法確定是哪一種修圖」 because 確定 means both "certain" and "determine" (`docs/capstone/REPORT.md`, §9, item 4).
6. *Holdout reads.* The 361-image holdout was read five times during the design (Section 5.2).
7. *Browser model.* The page runs a smaller model that agrees with the main model on 43 of 50 decisions, so it does not reproduce the numbers of Sections 3 to 5; the Python latencies were measured under CPU load and only their order of magnitude is claimed (`HOLDOUT_FINAL.md`, §4).
8. *Human study.* Small, exploratory, pending; its participants are students, its images are twelve, its beautified items are FFHQ-based only, and the advisor was not consulted on ethics review before it started (Section 6.3).

## 8. Conclusion

Fake-face detectors only say real or fake, so they call a skin-smoothed real face fake. The audit of seventeen published detectors shows the error in both directions, the training experiment traces the accusation to the absence of skin smoothing on both classes during training, and the edge-treatment experiment names what the detectors react to, a face smoother than its own context. The system built here says real, forged or just beautified, circles its reason on the face, and shows only what code has checked, because the reasons a model writes for itself match their own photograph about as often as a coin flip. Each promise has a test that could have failed: the controlled comparison for the answer, the restoration test for the place, the checker's catches for the sentences. The costs are stated beside the answer: the third class adds a label rather than detection, beautified deepfakes can hide in it, untouched photographs are sometimes called beautified, and only smoothing can be named. Whether any of this helps a person judge a face is the question the pre-registered study will answer.
